A WhatsApp ordering assistant for Ugandan restaurants

Summary
  • WhatsApp is close to free, but not completely. The Cloud API itself costs nothing and no reseller is needed. From 1 Oct 2026, each number gets 1,000 free service replies a month, and each reply after that costs about $0.004 in Uganda. A busy restaurant sending 3,000 replies a month would pay about $8.
  • "Meta AI" isn't the free part. Meta Business Agent costs $2 per million tokens through the API, which works out to about 4–5¢ per message, and it's limited to eligible partners. Our own AI agent costs under 1¢ for a whole order conversation.
  • Luganda works, through a Ugandan provider. Whisper, OpenAI, Groq and Azure don't support Luganda speech. Sunbird AI does: it has a Luganda speech-to-text API that accepts WhatsApp's OGG audio and has a free developer tier. ElevenLabs Scribe is the paid fallback.
  • Nobody local does this yet. Jumia Food left Uganda in 2023. Glovo and SafeBoda take commission. The one AI WhatsApp agent aimed at Uganda, HustleFlow ($15–100/mo), isn't built for restaurants and doesn't mention Luganda.
  • Two rules to follow. WhatsApp bans general-purpose AI chatbots, so the bot must stay on the menu and orders. Uganda's data law requires registration with its data protection office (UGX 100,000 a year) and consent before chats are stored abroad.

Limits that shape the build

  • Test number: Meta's free test number can message about 5 verified phones. That's enough for us to test, but not for a public demo, so we're building a web simulator for that.
  • New accounts start at 250 business-initiated conversations a day. Replying to customers who message first isn't limited this way.
  • Each restaurant on its own number requires Meta's Tech Provider programme (business verification and app review) plus Embedded Signup. The default cap is 10 new customers a week, or 200 after verification.
  • Coexistence lets a restaurant keep using its existing WhatsApp Business app number alongside the API. It also requires Tech Provider status, and catalogs and orders don't work on those numbers.
  • No official Node SDK: Meta archived it in 2023, so we call the Graph API with fetch.
  • Voice notes arrive as audio/ogg; codecs=opus. The media download link expires after 5 minutes.
  • Menus as messages: list messages take up to 10 rows and reply buttons up to 3. Images must be JPEG or PNG, up to 5 MB.

The AI chatbot rule

Since 15 Jan 2026, WhatsApp's terms bar AI providers whose assistant is the "primary functionality", meaning ChatGPT-style bots that answer anything. AI that's "ancillary to a legitimate business service" is allowed: confirming orders, tracking, bookings and FAQs. Our bot has to politely refuse anything that isn't about the restaurant.

All the major models understand Luganda reasonably well. Gemini and Sunflower write it noticeably better than GPT-4o. Sunflower has an OpenAI-compatible API, but its docs don't mention tool calling, so it can't take orders on its own. We use Gemini Flash as the main model and keep Sunflower as an optional "make this sound natural in Luganda" pass. No published benchmark covers the cheaper Flash and mini models in Luganda, so we'll test our own set of about 30 real messages.

Luganda voice replies for later: Sunbird has a text-to-speech API with 8 Luganda voices. Meta's MMS-TTS is licensed for non-commercial use only.

Today in Kampala, people call or WhatsApp the restaurant, a boda rider delivers, and they pay by MTN MoMo or cash. The pain for restaurants is re-typing chat orders, missed messages at peak times, and marketplace commission. Where we can stand out:

  • Luganda voice notes and texts
  • a live order board
  • a bot workflow the owner can edit
  • no commission
  • mobile money later

Pricing anchor: local tools cost about $6–14 a month, and HustleFlow charges $15–50.

Payments (later phase)

  • MTN MoMo Open API:
    • Free to join, with a "RequestToPay" collections API.
    • The sandbox only works in EUR.
    • Going live needs KYC documents, and forum reports mention broken signup links and upload-size limits.
    • MTN has about 54% of mobile-money subscriptions (unverified).
  • Airtel Money: developers.airtel.africa offers collections, with KYC for production. Fees aren't published.
  • Aggregators (fastest path):
    • Flutterwave Uganda charges 3% on mobile-money collections and 4.8% on cards, with next-day settlement.
    • Relworx covers MTN and Airtel with good docs, but doesn't publish fees.
    • Pesapal is about 3.5%.
    • Yo! Payments needs incorporation papers and an NDA.
  • Taxes: a 0.5% mobile-money withdrawal levy, 12% excise on data and 18% VAT. There's no WhatsApp-specific tax; the 2018 social-media tax was replaced in 2021.

Data protection

  • Registration: the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 (s.29) requires every data collector, processor and controller to register with the PDPO, whatever its size. It costs UGX 100,000 and is renewed every year. The first conviction for not registering came in July 2025, a UGX 300,000 fine.
  • Data abroad: s.19 requires protection at least equal to Ugandan law, plus consent, when data leaves Uganda. The PDPO found Google in breach in July 2025. Our chats live in Supabase and pass through US AI providers, so the bot's first message includes a short consent line and a link to the privacy notice.
  • This is research, not legal advice. Restaurants using the platform may need to register too.

Build choices that follow from this

  • Transport: WhatsApp Cloud API directly, with no BSP, one number for now, and a web simulator as the public demo.
  • Brain: our own AI SDK agent that follows an editable workflow graph, using Gemini 2.5 Flash through the Vercel AI Gateway with GPT-4o-mini as fallback. Prices and totals always come from the database, never from the model.
  • Voice notes: Sunbird first (lug or eng), then ElevenLabs Scribe, then "please type it".
  • Order alerts: a Supabase Realtime dashboard with sound and a browser notification. No email service needed.
  • Workflow editor: @xyflow/react 12 (MIT, React 19) with React Flow UI shadcn components and dagre layout. The nodes and edges follow ElevenLabs' design: stages with their own instructions, and conditions written in plain language.

Unknowns

  • Meta's official Rest of Africa rate card after 1 Oct 2026. The partner figures agree with each other, but Meta's page hasn't caught up.
  • Sunbird's rate limits, uptime and commercial pricing. Ask them before launch.
  • How well any speech service handles Luganda–English code-switching. There's no independent benchmark, so we'll test on real notes.
  • How the cheaper Gemini Flash and GPT mini models perform in Luganda. Only the larger Pro and 4o models have been measured.
  • Glovo and SafeBoda's restaurant commission in Uganda, and fees at MTN, Airtel, Relworx and Pesapal.
  • Whether Meta Business Agent is available in Uganda and can take orders.
